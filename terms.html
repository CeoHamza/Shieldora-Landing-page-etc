5. What Shieldora does

Once you link your child’s iPhone, Shieldora:

  • filters websites through our encrypted DNS resolver, which uses Cloudflare’s 1.1.1.1 for Families to block adult content, malware, phishing and other categories Cloudflare’s family filter covers, and forces safe search on Google, Bing and DuckDuckGo unless you turn it off;
  • applies Apple’s Screen Time controls you choose: device restrictions, Safari’s web-content filter, and blocking of other browsers;
  • lets you block piracy streaming sites using a list we maintain;
  • lets you block Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, Discord and Reddit, the apps and their websites, at all times or on a schedule you set;
  • tells you when a site is blocked on your child’s phone, and when protection stops running (for example the phone stops checking in, web filtering appears to be off, or a protection is switched off); and
  • if you turn on detailed block alerts for a child, shows you the domain of a blocked site (for example “example.com”, never the full page, path or search) in the app only, for 30 days.

How each of these handles information is set out in the Privacy Policy.

5.1 Where the block lists come from

Cloudflare’s family filter. Most blocking decisions are made by Cloudflare’s 1.1.1.1 for Families service. Its categories and the sites in them are decided and updated by Cloudflare, automatically. We do not choose, review or edit Cloudflare’s list, and Cloudflare does not tell us why a site was blocked. That is why a block is shown to you as “Family filter” or “Security risk”, not as “Adult”. Cloudflare explains how the service works at developers.cloudflare.com/1.1.1.1 and how it handles lookups in its public resolver privacy policy.

If you think a site has been blocked wrongly, tell us. We keep a short reviewed allow list of domains we have checked and found wrongly classed (see the Privacy Policy, 3.3), and we can pass reports on to Cloudflare, but we cannot promise any site will be unblocked.

Shieldora’s piracy list. The piracy streaming list is our own list, built from HaGeZi’s public Anti-Piracy list and refreshed by us weekly after a check. It will not catch every piracy site, and it can occasionally include a site that should not be on it.

Social app lists. The websites blocked for each social app come from a short list we publish in the app.

6. What Shieldora can’t do

We would rather you hear this from us than find out later.

6.1 No filter is perfect

Filters miss things and sometimes block things they should not. New sites appear every day. Shieldora lowers the chance your child sees harmful content and tells you when protection stops working. It does not guarantee your child will never see something harmful, and it is not a substitute for supervision, talking with your child, and age-appropriate rules.

6.2 Apps we can’t reach

Web filtering works on web lookups, mainly in Safari. It does not reach content served inside other apps, for example videos inside YouTube or messages inside Telegram, Signal, WhatsApp or similar apps. We can block only the five social apps listed in section 5. Any other app is not blocked by Shieldora, and if your child can install apps they may install one that is not on that list. Use Apple’s app installation restriction if you want to stop that.

If you allow a third-party browser, content inside it is not filtered. The app tells you this when you allow one.

6.3 What a block alert means

A block alert means a site was blocked on your child’s phone. It does not mean your child went looking for it. Pop-ups, ads and links inside other sites and apps cause many blocks. When several sites are blocked within seconds, the app tells you it was probably pop-ups or ads.

6.4 Protection can be switched off

A determined child, or someone else with the phone, may be able to switch off parts of the protection, for example by removing Shieldora’s DNS setting, turning on a VPN, removing Screen Time permission, or deleting the app if the device restrictions you set allow it. Shieldora is designed to tell you when this happens (for example “web filtering may be off” or “protection is offline”), so you can follow up. We cannot stop every way around it, and an alert can be delayed if the phone is off or has no signal.

6.5 Delays and outages

The service depends on Apple, Cloudflare, our hosting providers and your phone’s internet connection. Alerts may be delayed or not arrive, for example if notifications are turned off on your phone or a provider has an outage. If our own resolver is unreachable, the child’s phone falls back to Cloudflare’s family filter directly, so filtering continues, but block alerts pause until our resolver is back.
