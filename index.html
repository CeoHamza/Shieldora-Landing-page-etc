The problem

FAQ

Questions parents ask

What exactly do the alerts tell me?

Whether protection is running. Three things: it went offline, it came back, or a browser was allowed — each with the time and the device. Nothing about what your child did.

Do you see my child's browsing history or messages?

No. We don't collect search terms, typed text, messages or browsing history. That's a deliberate line, not a feature we haven't built yet.

Can my child just turn it off?

No. There is no Shieldora screen that turns protection off, and the app can't be deleted. Lifting it means going into iOS Settings, where iOS asks for the parent's Apple Account password — not the child's device passcode or Face ID. And if protection is ever switched off, you get an alert, so it can't happen quietly.

Will you tell me what my child tried to visit?

Not today, and possibly not ever. Apple’s filter doesn’t report individual blocks to us, so there is nothing to tell you. If that changes it will be because we added a filtering layer of our own, and we’d say so plainly here first.

Will it catch everything?

No filter is perfect, and some content will get through. What we can promise is different and, we think, more useful: you will know if the filter stops running — which is the failure that actually leaves a child unprotected, and the one most filters never mention.

How does the blocking actually work?

Shieldora uses filtering built into iOS, on your child's iPhone or iPad. The filtering happens on the device itself.

Which browsers does it cover?

Safari, including private browsing. Other browsers are blocked by default. You can allow one if your child needs it, and we'll warn you that content inside it isn't filtered.
