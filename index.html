The problem

Nothing tells you when it matters

Three things go wrong in almost every family that hands a child a device.

You're the last to know

Something happens on a screen in another room and nothing reaches you. Most parents find out long after — if they find out at all.

Protection gets switched off in silence

A filter a child can quietly disable isn't protection. It fails without a sound, and nothing arrives to tell you it stopped.

The apps either do nothing or read everything

One end of the market is a toggle that changes little. The other logs messages and browsing. Neither is what most families actually want.

The solution

You get told. That's the whole point.

The moment a blocked site is hit, or protection is switched off, a notification lands on your phone — with the time and the device. You don't go looking. You get told.

The blocking underneath it uses filtering built into iOS, and it can't be turned off or deleted without your account password. That is what makes the alerts worth trusting: protection can't be quietly removed first, and if anyone tries, that's an alert too.

What we send is behaviour, not content — enough to start a conversation, without reading over your child's shoulder.

How it works

Three steps, then we do the work

Set it up on a weeknight and leave it alone.

1

Set up your parent account

You create it on your own phone. It's where every alert lands, and the only place protection can be changed.

2

Link your child's device

A guided setup switches on filtering built into iOS. Safari is covered, including private browsing. Other browsers are blocked by default — you can allow one, with a warning.

3

The alerts start

From then on it runs quietly, and you hear from Shieldora on the two things that matter: a blocked site was hit, or protection changed.

Being straight with you

What Shieldora doesn't do

None of this is missing work. Each one is a line we drew on purpose.

  • We don't see search terms, typed text, messages or browsing history. An alert tells you a blocked site was hit. It never tells you what your child typed to get there.
  • We store that something was blocked, not what it was. The record is that a block happened, and when. The page behind it isn't kept, so it can't be read back later — by you, by us, or by anyone else.
  • We don't show you your child's screen. No screenshots, no live view, no scrolling through their day. If that's what you're after, Shieldora is the wrong app.
  • We don't send you everything. Alerts are limited to the two things worth interrupting you for: a block happened, or protection changed. Everything else stays off your phone.

FAQ

Questions parents ask

What exactly do the alerts tell me?

Behaviour, not content. That a blocked site was hit, or that protection was turned off or came back on, with the time and the device.

Do you see my child's browsing history or messages?

No. We don't collect search terms, typed text, messages or browsing history. That's a deliberate line, not a feature we haven't built yet.

Can my child just turn it off?

No. Turning off protection or deleting the app needs your account password. And if protection is ever switched off, you get an alert — so it can't happen quietly.

Will it catch everything?

No filter is perfect, and some content will get through. That is exactly why the alerts matter: you hear what was blocked and when protection changed, rather than assuming nothing ever got past.

How does the blocking actually work?

Shieldora uses filtering built into iOS, on your child's iPhone or iPad. The filtering happens on the device itself.

Which browsers does it cover?

Safari, including private browsing. Other browsers are blocked by default. You can allow one if your child needs it, and we'll warn you that content inside it isn't filtered.
