When an adult site is blocked, protection is turned off, web filtering may be off, a browser is allowed, or your child’s phone goes quiet, a notification lands on your phone with the child, the device and the time. You don’t have to go and check.

Alerts never name the site.

A phone going quiet usually just means it’s switched off, flat, out of signal, or that iOS has paused Shieldora’s background checks, so that one is a gentle heads-up after 24 hours, not an alarm. Screen Time being turned off is the one that matters. Shieldora tries to tell you, usually within 20 minutes. If that alert can’t get through, the phone goes quiet, and 24 hours of silence always reaches you.

And it can’t be quietly removed first: there is nothing in Shieldora on your child’s phone that turns protection off, and lifting it in iOS Settings needs the parent’s Apple Account password, not the child’s passcode, not their Face ID.

To make sure alerts get through, Check alerts reach you in Settings sends you a test alert and asks you to confirm it arrived.

Who’s behind this

Most parental controls fail the same way. The filter gets switched off, or a kid gets around it, and the parent is the last to know. Sometimes that’s weeks later, and by then the conversation they should have had is long overdue. Shieldora exists to fix that one problem: block adult sites, and make sure you get told when something is blocked or protection stops.

Questions parents ask

What exactly do the alerts tell me?

The exceptions that matter: a site being blocked (including likely pop-ups or redirects), Screen Time being turned off, web filtering possibly being off, the phone going quiet for 24 hours, or you allowing a non-Safari browser. Each comes with the child, the device and the time. Nothing about what your child searched or typed.

Do you see my child's browsing history or messages?

No. We don't collect search terms, typed text, messages, or a history of sites that weren't blocked. That's a deliberate line, not a feature we haven't built yet. In the app, Settings has a screen called What Shieldora can't see that lists it all.

Can my child just turn it off?

No. Nothing in Shieldora on your child's phone turns protection off, and the app can't be deleted. Lifting it means going into iOS Settings, where iOS asks for the parent's Apple Account password, not the child's device passcode or Face ID. And if protection is switched off, Shieldora tries to alert you, usually within 20 minutes. If that signal does not get through, the phone falls silent, and silence that lasts 24 hours reaches you on its own, so a switch-off does not stay hidden.

Will you tell me what my child tried to visit?

No. A block alert names the child, the device and the time, never the site. We never record searches, pages or messages.

Will it catch everything?

No filter is perfect, and some content will get through. What we focus on is different and, we think, more useful: if the filtering stops running, Shieldora tries to tell you, and a phone that goes quiet for 24 hours will reach you on its own. That is the failure that actually leaves a child exposed, and the one most filters never mention.

How does the blocking actually work?

Two layers. Your child's iPhone sends its website lookups through Shieldora's filter in Sydney, which blocks adult and harmful sites. The adult filter built into iOS stays on as a backup. We don't keep a list of the sites your child visits.

Which browsers does it cover?

Safari, including private browsing. Other browsers are blocked by default. You can allow one if your child needs it, and the app tells you that filtering may not hold inside it.

What do I need to use it?

Your child’s iPhone or iPad needs to be on iOS 26.5 or later. That’s the version where Shieldora can watch for Screen Time being turned off, on top of noticing when the phone goes quiet. Your own phone can be on an older version. If your child’s device is behind, Shieldora asks you to update it before setting it up, rather than guard it in a way it can’t stand behind.
